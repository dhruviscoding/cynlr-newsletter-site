KRKassow Robotics

CynLr and Kassow Robotics, on-site with the arm under discussion.

Press-fitting looks easy. It isn't.

Tasks like press-fitting two parts together — the single most common operation in assembling mobile phones and plastic components — sound trivial. They aren't. They require force sensitivity between arms, synchronized force negotiation, and precise control that most robotic kinematics still can't deliver.

Kassow Robotics founder Kristian Kassow met with CynLr's Gokul N A, Dr. Michael Bombile, and Kaifali Bhojani to talk through exactly this gap — and what it would take to close it. Kassow is now exploring a relationship with CynLr that goes beyond the standard OEM arrangement: early access to CynLr's development on top of their own platform, including multi-arm coordination.

URUniversal Robots

The UR lineup, and the layer CynLr is building on top of it.

UR is building CynLr into its own stack

Following a prior meeting with UR CEO Kim Povlsen, VP of Technology Anders Beck invited CynLr back to go deeper: UR wants to onboard CynLr to build the software layers that let their robots handle unknown objects, unpredictable forces, and multi-arm coordination — the problems their current stack can't reach on its own.

UR is also looking to pair this with its existing Nvidia partnership, integrating CynLr's reinforcement-learning work given CynLr's prior contributions to the Nvidia stack. This isn't a pilot. It's UR treating CynLr as infrastructure.

KKUKA

of global robotics demand sits in automotive and electronics — the two industries KUKA named as its next target.

Eye-hand coordination, at KUKA's request

At KUKA's China headquarters, CTO Hui Zhang and board members outlined plans to move into intricate assembly across automotive and electronics. Separately, Christian Felkel, KUKA's Head of Global Business, raised the same need during CynLr's Swiss facility launch: robots with genuine eye-hand coordination for India's expanding manufacturing base.

Two conversations, continents apart, converging on the same request — that's a stronger signal than either one alone. Both point the same direction: co-developing robots that adapt to new scenarios without special retraining.

CynLr's team has been invited to Doosan's Seoul headquarters to meet CEO William Ryu and the board. The agenda: standardizing robot arms for CyRo, and finding an R&D partner willing to help build missing pieces of that engine.
